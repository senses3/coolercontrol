# What's New in CoolerControl 5.0

CoolerControl 5.0 is the largest release in the project's history: a rebuilt interface, a simple
mode for newcomers, wizards that do the setup for you, and a faster daemon underneath. Here is
what's new.

## The new interface

The application shell was rebuilt from the ground up around six areas: Home, Cooling, Monitoring,
Devices, Plugins and Settings. A persistent icon rail gets you anywhere in one click, each section
has its own side panel, and every area opens with a landing page that shows what matters at a
glance.

![The new interface](images/5-0/home-full.webp)

## Simple mode

CoolerControl now greets new setups with a Simple interface: pick a fan, draw a curve, done. There
are no profiles, functions or modes to learn until you want them; the full interface stays one
header toggle away, and switching is lossless in both directions.

![Simple mode](images/5-0/simple-fans.webp)

## Auto-Create Profiles

A new wizard inspects your hardware and generates a sensible cooling setup for CPU coolers, GPU
fans, AIO pumps and radiators, case fans and laptops. What used to be an evening of tinkering is now
a working configuration in under a minute, and everything it creates is a normal profile you can
tune afterwards.

![Auto-Create Profiles](images/5-0/auto-create.webp)

## Fan calibration

The calibration wizard measures what your fans actually do: their true minimum, where they stall,
and how duty maps to RPM. Firmware-controlled fans are recognized and mapped through calibration
too, and a calibrated fan's settings can be converted to true duty with one click.

![Fan calibration](images/5-0/calibration.webp)

## Device health and hardware insight

CoolerControl now tells you how your hardware is doing and why. A health panel tracks failsafed
channels and missing sensors, with badges through the menus, while the new Hardware Support card on
Home explains what startup detection found, why a fan cannot be controlled, and produces a
paste-ready hardware report for bug reports.

![Device health](images/5-0/device-health.webp)

## On your phone

The Web UI now has a real mobile layout: bottom navigation, a dashboard switcher, and editors that
reflow to fit. Check temperatures or fix a curve from the couch.

![On your phone](images/5-0/mobile.webp)

## Themes

Nine color themes ship installed, in light and dark variants: True Black, Dracula, Gruvbox, One,
Catppuccin, Tokyo Night, Monokai, Nord and Solarized, all chosen from a preview dropdown. Custom
themes gained success, warning, error and info colors, and are shareable with a single theme code.

![Themes](images/5-0/themes.webp)

## The desktop app grows up

The desktop app now manages a list of saved daemon connections with per-connection access tokens,
switches daemons from a tray submenu, and pins live sensor readings straight into the tray menu.
Connections to remote daemons are protected by TLS certificate pinning with a first-use trust
prompt.

![The desktop app](images/5-0/tray.webp)

## Alerts that work with you

Alerts can now watch multiple sources at once with per-source state, be silenced for a set time with
a notification when the silence expires, and keep a log on each alert page. An indicator in the top
bar and a tray badge make sure you still notice the ones that matter.

![Alerts](images/5-0/alerts.webp)

## Under the hood

The daemon now runs on io_uring by default, holds sysfs descriptors across ticks, and reads sensors
without heap allocation. LCD updates cost a fraction of what they did, the UI and desktop app each
use a single connection for live updates, log output is bounded and de-duplicated, and the
long-session memory growth in the desktop app is fixed.

...and much more. See the [full changelog](../../CHANGELOG.md) for everything in 5.0.
